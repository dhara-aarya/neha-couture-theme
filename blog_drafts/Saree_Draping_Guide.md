## How to Drape a Saree: A Step-by-Step Guide for Beginners

Draping a saree is an elegant art form that has been passed down through generations. However, if you are living in Europe or attending your first Indian wedding in Germany, the six yards of fabric can seem intimidating.

### Step 1: Gather Your Essentials
Before you begin, ensure you have the following three components ready:
1. **The Saree:** The beautiful 6-yard fabric itself.
2. **The Blouse:** A well-fitted crop top. (Check out our [Readymade Stitched Blouses](/collections/blouse-it-completes-the-best-dress-ever-made-for-a-women) if you don't have one).
3. **The Petticoat (Underskirt):** A secure skirt worn underneath to hold the drape in place. Our [Saree Shapewear & Petticoats](/collections/shape-your-sarees-with-stylish-underskirts-and-petticoats) provide the perfect structured foundation.
4. **Safety Pins:** Essential for beginners to keep everything secure!
5. **Your Shoes:** *Always put your heels on before draping* so you get the length exactly right.

### Step 2: The Initial Tuck
Start with the plain end of the saree (the part without the heavy border or *pallu*). 
Tuck the top edge of this plain end into your petticoat, starting slightly to the right of your navel. Ensure the bottom edge of the saree is barely sweeping the floor.
Slowly wrap the saree around your waist once, making a complete circle, and tuck it in securely all the way around.

### Step 3: Measuring the Pallu
Take the decorative end of the saree (the *pallu*). Bring it around your back and drape it over your **left shoulder**. 
Adjust the length so that it falls at least to the back of your knees. Don't pin it just yet—let it rest on your shoulder while you make the front pleats.

### Step 4: Making the Pleats
This is the most crucial step! Gather the remaining loose fabric in front of you. 
Using your thumb and index finger, fold the fabric into neat pleats (about 4 to 5 inches wide each). You should aim for about 5 to 7 pleats. 
Hold the gathered pleats together tightly and secure them with a safety pin at the top. 

### Step 5: Tucking the Pleats
Tuck the pinned pleats neatly into the center of your petticoat, right below your navel. Ensure that the pleats are facing to your **left**. 
Smooth out the fabric so it sits flat against your stomach.

### Step 6: Securing the Pallu
Finally, return to the *pallu* resting on your left shoulder. You can either leave it flowing freely for a modern look, or gather it into neat shoulder pleats for a traditional, structured finish.
Pin the pallu securely to the fabric of your blouse at the top of your shoulder.
